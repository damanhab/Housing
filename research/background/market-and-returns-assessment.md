# Workers' Accommodation / Group Housing (السكن الجماعي للأفراد / سكن العمال) in Saudi Arabia: Market & Returns Assessment for Riyadh and Dammam/Khobar

> **Revision log**
> - 2026-10-06: Updated Mnzil's profile (scale, SAR 1bn fund, pricing). Added Mnzil's actual price point to bed rates. Added that paying a housing allowance does not exempt an employer from licensing (Al-Watan, 29 Dec 2025). Flagged the capex benchmark as contested by the Mnzil fund's implied cost.

## TL;DR
- **This is an operating business, not passive rent.** Purpose-built worker accommodation (PBWA) in Riyadh and Dammam/Khobar can be built for roughly SAR 55,000–60,000 per bed and generates gross rent of ~SAR 4,800–8,400/bed/year (SAR 400–700/bed/month), but roughly 30–45% of revenue is consumed by opex (utilities, catering, security, housekeeping, transport, management) — so its headline "yield" is NOT comparable to a residential apartment's ~9% gross yield unless opex is stripped out.
- **Demand is being pushed up hard by a new mandatory licensing regime.** Per SPA (17 September 2025), the National Program for the Development of Collective Housing for Individuals called on all establishments employing 20 or more workers to obtain a Balady collective-housing license matching capacity, enforced via the Balady–Qiwa link (non-compliance suspends the commercial registration and bars visa-quota expansion and worker transfers). This sits on top of a ~3.4 million construction workforce and giga-project activity. But the user's deliberate restriction of Eastern Province scope to Dammam/Khobar excludes Jubail and Ras Al Khair, where over 90,000 industrial beds of demand actually sit.
- **Keep the two return models separate:** an OWNERSHIP (develop-and-hold) play targets a stabilized net yield in the mid-to-high single digits on ~SAR 56k/bed capex; a LEASE-AND-OPERATE play targets a cash-on-cash return on much smaller fit-out/working capital but carries acute occupancy and counterparty risk tied to project cycles.

## Key Findings

1. **Market structure is bifurcated** between (a) purpose-built specialist compounds run by professional operators (SMASCO, Mnzil, Namariq, Mawref/Laam, Saudi Iwaa, Himmah/Dammam Village, Anan Iskan) and (b) a very large stock of converted residential/commercial buildings ("عمائر سكن عمال") rented per-building or per-room, advertised on Aqar, Haraj, Mstaml and expatriates.com. The converted stock still dominates by bed count; the regulatory regime is deliberately pushing demand toward the compliant purpose-built segment.

2. **The mandatory licensing regime is the single most important demand driver.** From September 2025, MOMAH's National Program made licensed group housing compulsory for establishments with 20+ workers, phased large→medium→small, with the Balady–Qiwa electronic link suspending the commercial registration of non-compliant establishments. Per Al Riyadh Daily, MOMAH has since issued more than 1,360 group-housing licenses, with licensed capacity reaching around one million beneficiaries. This converts a welfare aspiration into a hard compliance purchase.

3. **Capex benchmark for worker (blue-collar) housing is ~SAR 55,750/bed**, derived directly from the SARCC (PIF unit)/Mawref project: per SARCC's official release (9 Feb 2026), a non-binding Heads of Terms with MAWREF Company to develop a **12,000-bed worker accommodation project in North Riyadh** on ~120,000 m² of land, 150,000 m² built-up area, at an estimated development cost of **SAR 669 million** (first phase of operations expected Q1 2029) — i.e. SAR 55,750/bed and SAR 4,460/m². A parallel SARCC/Tamimi Global "staff accommodation" deal (per Gulf Construction Online, Feb 2026: 4,000 beds, 142,000 m² plot, 201,000 m² BUA, **SAR 1.52 billion**) implies ~SAR 380,000/bed — the ~7x gap is product-tier (managerial/staff vs. worker bunk housing), so the user must be clear which tier they are underwriting. The two HoTs together are worth SAR 2.2 billion ($586.3m).

4. **Prevailing bed rates: SAR 400–700/bed/month via a provider in Riyadh; SAR 200–400/bed/month for a self-managed leased building** (excluding the operator's own management team costs), per Mnzil. Catering adds materially: facility-management-only outsourcing runs ~SAR 50–150/bed/month, rising to ~SAR 200–400/bed/month with catering included.

5. **The only explicit Saudi labour-housing return figure found is a ~7.83% yield** for the "Labor Housing" category in a Saudi REIT dataset (year-end 2024, an Estater proptech-blog dataset) — essentially in line with Riyadh residential gross yields. Per Global Property Guide's Saudi Arabia Residential Property Market Analysis 2026, citing the STC Real Estate Index (Stephane Tajick Consulting), the average gross rental yield was **8.89% in Riyadh** and 7.89% in Jeddah, with the national average at 6.84%. No Tier-1 consultancy (JLL, Knight Frank, CBRE, Colliers, Savills) publishes an asset-level cap rate for Saudi worker accommodation; they cover only the regulatory framework and residential yields.

6. **Eastern Province demand is concentrated outside the user's chosen scope.** The Second Industrial City in Dammam alone has a worker residential zone of 202 buildings on ~1 million m² housing ~25,000 workers (per MODON's spokesman, cited by SPA/Al-Watan); RCJY reported ~93,273 workers across Jubail and Ras Al Khair industrial cities, with Jubail Industrial City having ~116,000 workers and 11 dedicated worker-housing districts. Dammam/Khobar proper is the retail/converted-stock and light-industrial submarket; the heavy industrial bed demand sits in Jubail, Ras Al Khair and SPARK.

## Details

### B5 — Size and structure (Riyadh; Dammam/Khobar)

There is **no reliable published total bed count** for Riyadh or Dammam/Khobar specifically; this asset class is thinly reported because most transactions are private master leases. What can be sourced:

- **National framing (use with caution):** trade commentary describes a program to "close a four-million-bed gap by converting two million unorganized accommodations into organized housing." This is a directional policy narrative from secondary commentary (a Substack analysis of Mnzil), not an official statistic, and should be treated as an order-of-magnitude claim only.
- **Riyadh** is described by operators (Mnzil) as the largest worker-housing market in the Kingdom, treated as four zones (North/South/East/West). Clusters are in the industrial/commercial corridors — Al Sulay (السلي, near Exits 17–18), Al Masani' (حي المصانع), the Kharj Road corridor, and Al Aridh/Al Areedh (العارض) in the north. Purpose-built examples: Mawref's LAAM 1 (opened Q1 2016) and LAAM 2 in Riyadh; SARCC's announced 12,000-bed (Mawref) and 4,000-bed (Tamimi Global) North Riyadh projects; Saudi Iwaa's Riyadh and Kharj compounds (the Kharj compound: 222 rooms, ~1,110-bed capacity, plus a second phase under construction).
- **Dammam/Khobar:** the retail market is visible on portals (Second Industrial City / Al Khaleej / Al Nour / Al Nawras districts in Dammam; "Madinat Al Ummal / مدينة العمال" districts in both Dammam and Khobar). Purpose-built: Himmah Consortium's "Dammam Village" (قرية الدمام) — 7 four-storey buildings on 17,000 m² serving the Second/Third Industrial Cities and SPARK, Aramco- and group-housing-compliant.

- **Main operators (named, with disclosed scale):**
  - **SMASCO (Saudi Manpower Solutions Co., Tadawul 1834)** — listed manpower group (IPO May 2024, 30% float, SAR 400m capital, offer price SAR 7.5/share); accommodation is an internal, asset-light function (leases most accommodation; owns the "Al Aridh" prototype complex). Per GIB Capital equity research (June 2024), "Housing & Food" is only ~2% of cost of revenue and the accommodation-carrying subsidiary contributed ~4–6% of group topline. **Not a pure worker-housing landlord** and does not disclose per-bed accommodation economics; useful mainly for macro backdrop and its ~13% group gross margin.
  - **Mnzil** — Riyadh-based workforce-housing proptech; founded 2024 by Abdulmajeed Al-Babtain and Abdulrahman Al-Shaya; $11.7m (SAR 44m) Series A led by Founders Fund, with COTU Ventures participating (Nov 2025). **As of Oct 2026: 35,000+ workers, ~250 corporate clients, 32+ cities** (mnzil.com; fund press release), with clients including Amazon, Noon, Nestlé, McDonald's, Americana, SIXT and SMASCO. On **5 Oct 2026** it launched a **SAR 1bn fund** with The Development Group (TDG) and Capital Partners to build compounds for up to 50,000 workers in Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, Khobar, Makkah and Madinah, with Mnzil as exclusive operator. Construction starts Q4 2026. Price ≈ SAR 375–450 per worker per month. See `../competitor-mnzil.md`.
  - **Namariq** — 13+ years; portfolio exceeding 12,000 beds; ISO-certified integrated model (housing + catering + FM + transport); strong in Western/industrial zones.
  - **Mawref Logistics (MASIC subsidiary), brand "Laam"** — LAAM 1: 11 buildings, 1,277 rooms, 6 shops, +7,500 labour capacity; also a +900-staff, 445-room block; LAAM 2 adds a three-floor central kitchen/dining (1,518 m²).
  - **Saudi Iwaa (إيواء السعودية)** — compounds in Riyadh, Jeddah, Madinah, Rabigh, Kharj, Makkah; a 1,500-bed Madinah industrial-city compound inaugurated by the Minister of Industry; 3× ISO certified; offers develop-build-operate and separate-service contracts.
  - **Anan Iskan (عنان إسكان)** — Ras Al Khair compound, 656 units on 75,000 m², 27 m²/person (managerial/staff tier).
  - **Himmah Consortium** — Dammam Village.

### B6 — Demand trend 2023 → Sept 2026 and forward

**Drivers up:**
- **Mandatory licensing (Sept 2025 onward)** — see Key Finding 2; the Balady–Qiwa link that suspends the CR (and blocks visa-quota expansion and worker transfers) is the enforcement teeth. MOMAH has issued 1,360+ licenses reaching ~one million licensed beneficiaries. **Paying workers a monthly housing allowance does not exempt the establishment from the licence requirement** (Al-Watan, 29 Dec 2025). Companies that solved housing with cash allowances therefore also fall inside the addressable market.
- **Construction workforce scale:** per Mahad Manpower Research's reading of the GASTAT Q4 2025 Labour Force Survey, the total construction workforce is ~3.4 million, of which 92% are non-Saudi expatriates (Indians 28%, Bangladeshis 18%, Pakistanis 16%), forecast to peak at ~4.1 million in 2029. Giga-project peak construction demand is separately estimated at 1.5–2 million workers.
- **Expatriate population:** ~15.7 million, or 44.4% of the population, per GASTAT 2024 (via Arab News). Total labour force ~16.9m, participation 67.4% (Q4 2025). Non-Saudi unemployment ~0.8% (Q1 2025) — near-full utilization implies housing demand tracks headcount tightly.
- **Industrial expansion & tenders:** MODON industrial cities, RCJY (Jubail/Yanbu/Ras Al Khair), SPARK, and new investment tenders — RCJY/Jabeen worker-housing agreements include Yanbu Al Sawari (4,000-worker, with Reem Al-Oula Trading), Jazan (5,450-worker, with Azhar Co.), Jubail (~7,680-worker), and a Ras Al Khair worker-housing plot of 258,282 m² offered west of the Abu Hadriyah road.

**Drivers down:**
- **Saudization/Nitaqat** — 2.48m Saudis entered the private sector since 2020; localization concentrated in supervisory/management roles (not bunk-housing demand) but progressively squeezes some expatriate categories.
- **Project completions and giga-project rescoping** — commentary flags giga-project rephasing risk (e.g., an oil-price-below-$60/bbl scenario cutting peak workforce). NEOM/giga rescoping in 2024–25 is a real downside to construction-phase bed demand at remote sites (less relevant to Riyadh/Dammam urban demand).
- **Riyadh 5-year rent freeze (from Sept 2025)** — caps residential rent escalation; a forward risk to income growth on any residentially-classified stock.

Net assessment: for Riyadh and Dammam/Khobar urban compliant supply, the licensing mandate is the dominant force and is demand-accretive in the near term.

### B7 — Where demand concentrates; where compliant supply can go

Regulation restricts siting to commercially-zoned plots on approved commercial streets (≥20 m wide), or within workshop/warehouse/light-industrial/car-showroom plan boundaries; requires 300 m separation from schools and hospitals; and explicitly instructs municipalities to avoid concentrating workers in particular districts. The compliant development envelope is therefore narrow and is the binding constraint on supply.

- **Riyadh — busy/saturated:** Al Sulay (السلي), Al Masani' (المصانع), old Kharj Road (طريق الخرج) — dense converted and new "سكن عمال" buildings.
- **Riyadh — under-supplied / development frontier:** the northern corridor (Al Aridh/العارض) near new industrial and logistics zones, and the western Jeddah-highway corridor; compliant purpose-built stock is scarce relative to the compliance-driven demand surge. The Qiddiya/Muzahimiyah corridor west of Riyadh shows active project-driven demand.
- **Dammam/Khobar — busy/saturated:** Second Industrial City, Al Khaleej, Al Nour, Al Nawras, Madinat Al Ummal.
- **Dammam/Khobar — the strategic caveat:** genuine large-scale industrial bed demand sits in **Jubail, Ras Al Khair and SPARK** — outside the user's chosen scope. By deliberately excluding these, the venture is targeting the smaller, more fragmented urban/light-industrial half of the regional market and forgoing the larger, contract-anchored industrial half. This restriction is defensible (urban plays are less lumpy and less single-counterparty-dependent) but the user should make it consciously, not by default.

### B8 — Rates, occupancy, contract structures

- **Bed rates — market reality check:** Mnzil's own all-in price is reported at **~$100–120 (SAR 375–450) per worker per month**, depending on the bundle. For modelling, use **SAR 400–450** as the base case for a serviced shared bed from a professional provider. The range in Mnzil's published guide, quoted next, is a ceiling, not the typical price.
- **Bed rates (per Mnzil's published buyer guide, 2026):** Riyadh shared via provider **SAR 400–700/bed/month**; self-managed leased building **SAR 200–400/bed/month** (excl. management team); large compounds outside city center **SAR 300–500/bed/month**. Portal listings corroborate the low end and below (e.g., Riyadh Sulay company rooms advertised around SAR 450; individual bedspaces SAR 400–800). **No separate published Dammam/Khobar bed-rate series was found**; portal evidence suggests Dammam/Khobar sits at or slightly below Riyadh levels for equivalent product.
- **Tiering:** Basic (bare bunk, shared bath, no services) ≈ low end; Mid (furnished, A/C, Wi-Fi, cleaning) ≈ mid; Premium/"staff" (private/managerial rooms ~23–27 m², amenities) ≈ well above SAR 700 and up to serviced-apartment levels. Catering and transport are the main add-ons: FM-only ~SAR 50–150/bed/month; with catering ~SAR 200–400/bed/month. Mnzil's buyer guide gives a worked example: a company weighing a SAR 400/bed unit 40 km away vs. a SAR 550/bed unit near-site pays ~SAR 200/worker/month more in transport — so location and bundling, not headline rent, determine true cost.
- **Occupancy:** No published occupancy series exists for this asset class. It is project-cycle-dependent; the ~7.83% REIT "Labor Housing" yield implies stabilized but not full utilization. Treat occupancy as the single most important and least-observable variable.
- **Contract structures:** two dominant forms — (1) **master lease to a single contractor** (whole building/compound, fixed term), bundled or bare; (2) **per-bed retail** to multiple small firms/individuals. Bundled catering/transport is the norm for the professional-operator segment; bare accommodation is the norm for converted-stock landlords. Master-lease tenure norms and escalation clauses in this specific niche are **not publicly documented**; general Saudi commercial-lease practice is multi-year with annual escalation, but the user should verify per deal.

### C9 — OWNERSHIP model returns vs. residential benchmark

- **Capex/bed (worker tier):** ~**SAR 55,750** all-in (derived from SARCC/Mawref: SAR 669m ÷ 12,000 beds; ≈ SAR 4,460/m² on 150,000 m² BUA). **Contested:** the Mnzil/TDG fund (SAR 1bn for up to 50,000 workers) implies **~SAR 20,000 per worker**. Possible reasons: the fund is leveraged, land is leased rather than bought, or the product is denser and lighter. Use SAR 20–56k as the range until a real quote narrows it. **Staff/managerial tier:** up to ~SAR 380,000/bed (SARCC/Tamimi, SAR 1.52bn ÷ 4,000 beds) — a fundamentally different product. In RCJY/Jabeen industrial-city deals, **land is allocated by the authority**, materially reducing investor land capex vs. open-market Riyadh/Dammam plots (a structural advantage of the industrial-zone route the user is choosing NOT to pursue).
- **Gross yield (ownership) — my derivation:** at SAR 400–700/bed/month = SAR 4,800–8,400/bed/year on ~SAR 56,000/bed capex → **gross yield ~8.6–15%** (upper end assumes premium rate + low land cost). A realistic mid-case at SAR 500/bed on full ~SAR 56k capex ≈ **~10.7% gross**.
- **Net yield (ownership) — my derivation:** after a 30–45% opex load (see C11), stabilized **net yield ≈ 5–9%**, consistent with the sourced ~7.83% "Labor Housing" REIT figure (year-end 2024). Anchored to one published yield point; treat as a defensible range, not precision.
- **Residential benchmark (like-for-like, comparable location):** Riyadh apartment gross yield **8.89%** (STC Real Estate Index via Global Property Guide; national avg 6.84%; Riyadh apartment rents up 19.6% YoY to avg SAR 30,832 per JLL); 7–11% for apartments generally. Residential **net** yield is only ~1–2 points below gross because the landlord carries almost no opex (no catering, security, housekeeping, transport, or 24/7 management). **This is the crux:** a worker-housing gross yield of ~10–11% and a residential gross yield of ~9% look similar, but the worker asset's NET yield is dragged far below by its opex base while the residential net yield sits just under its gross. **Comparing headline yields is invalid; only the net-of-opex comparison is meaningful.**

### C10 — LEASE-AND-OPERATE model returns

- **Structure:** operator master-leases a whole building, furnishes/fits it out, then sublets per-bed at a spread.
- **Spread — my derivation:** self-managed leased-building cost ~SAR 200–400/bed/month vs. per-bed retail SAR 400–700/bed/month → **gross spread ~SAR 150–350/bed/month before opex**. The operator's task is to convert that spread into margin after utilities, staffing and vacancy.
- **Fit-out & working capital:** fit-out (beds, lockers, A/C, kitchen, safety systems) is a fraction of ground-up capex; because the shell is leased, the operator's own outlay is dominated by furniture/fixtures + deposits + first-months' rent + pre-opening. **No clean published SAR/bed fit-out-only figure exists for Saudi Arabia** — flag this as an estimate gap the user must price from a real building.
- **Cash-on-cash & payback:** because capital deployed is small relative to the ownership model, cash-on-cash can be high **when occupancy is strong**, but is extremely sensitive to occupancy and to the fixed master-lease rent, which must be paid whether beds are full or empty. **No sourced payback figure exists for this market**; it must be modelled per deal.
- **Lease tenure/escalation/renewal:** not publicly documented for this niche (see B8). The key asymmetry: the operator is short a fixed multi-year rent and long variable, project-linked occupancy income — a classic operating-leverage risk.

### C11 — Operating cost load (share of revenue)

This is the reason ownership and lease-operate yields are not comparable to residential rent. Indicative opex build (my estimate, anchored to sourced tariff and service-fee data; ranges reflect basic→catered product):
- **Utilities (electricity + water):** commercial electricity tariff ~20–32 halalas/kWh (a flat ~20 h/kWh is widely cited; a two-tier 22/32 h/kWh above 6,000 kWh/month is also reported), plus 15% VAT; SEC added 2 h/kWh for non-eligible commercial/industrial users from 28 May 2025. Water via the National Water Company is heavily subsidized but non-trivial at scale. Utilities are a major line in a high-A/C-load bunk building — materially higher per m² than a family apartment.
- **Catering:** the largest single variable line where bundled — roughly the difference between the FM-only (~SAR 50–150/bed/mo) and with-catering (~SAR 200–400/bed/mo) price points, i.e. catering alone can be ~SAR 150–250/bed/month.
- **Security (24/7), housekeeping, maintenance, management, transport:** together the balance of the FM fee; FM-only outsourcing at SAR 50–150/bed/month is a useful floor for these combined.
- **Total opex load:** **~30–45% of gross revenue** for a serviced product (my estimate; by comparison, Sands of Wealth cites a 25–35% all-in holding-cost ratio for *residential* — worker housing sits above this because of catering/security/transport). Residential landlords carry near-zero of this base — the essential point for the comparison.

### C12 — Risk asymmetries

- **Occupancy volatility (both, worse for lease-operate):** demand is project-cycle-linked; a contractor demobilizing empties beds fast. An owner can re-let over time; a lease-operator still owes fixed rent — the highest-beta position.
- **Counterparty concentration:** a single contractor filling an entire building is the market norm and the biggest single risk — one master-lease default or non-renewal can zero out an asset's income. Per-bed retail diversifies this but raises management intensity and turnover.
- **Regulatory reclassification:** the same licensing regime that creates demand can strand non-compliant assets (siting, density, catering-license, 300 m school/hospital setback). Buying/leasing a building that cannot be licensed to its assumed capacity is the key diligence trap — capacity is "assessed against actual occupancy, not design intent."
- **Exit liquidity / who buys:** the buyer pool is thin — specialist operators, industrial REITs, PIF-linked platforms (e.g., SARCC), and MASIC-type investors. No published resale cap rate exists; the ~7.83% REIT "Labor Housing" yield is the best available proxy for an institutional exit level, but a private single-asset resale would likely price wider (higher cap rate / lower value) to compensate for illiquidity and operating risk.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Decide the model and tier before committing capital.**
- If the goal is a compliance-driven annuity with institutional exit optionality, pursue **ownership of a purpose-built worker-tier asset** at ~SAR 56k/bed, underwriting a **net yield of ~6–8%**, and demand that the yield premium over residential (~9% gross / ~7–8% net) genuinely compensates for the opex burden and operating risk. If it does not, buy the apartment building instead — the residential net yield is easier and lower-risk.
- If capital is constrained and the team has operational capability, pursue **lease-and-operate**, but only with (a) a pre-signed anchor master-lease or LOI from a creditworthy contractor covering ≥60–70% of beds, and (b) a fixed-rent-to-projected-revenue ratio that survives a 30% occupancy drop.

**Stage 2 — Diligence gates (any deal):**
- Confirm the building can be **Balady-licensed to the assumed bed capacity** on its actual zoning (commercial street ≥20 m or light-industrial/warehouse boundary), with the 300 m school/hospital setback and no municipal concentration objection. Treat license capacity, not design capacity, as the revenue ceiling.
- Get a **real quote** for fit-out/bed and for the bundled FM+catering+transport cost/bed from an operator (Namariq, Saudi Iwaa, Mnzil) to replace the estimated opex load with actuals.
- Verify **master-lease tenure, escalation and notice terms** on the specific building — these are undocumented market-wide and must be nailed per deal.

**Stage 3 — Positioning:**
- Target the **under-supplied compliant frontier** (North Riyadh/Al Aridh corridor; compliant light-industrial plots near Dammam Second/Third Industrial City and SPARK access) rather than saturated Sulay/Masani' or Dammam Al Khaleej.
- **Reconsider the Jubail/Ras Al Khair exclusion.** Quantified demand there (~93,000+ industrial workers; RCJY/Jabeen tenders with authority-allocated land) is the larger, contract-anchored half of the Eastern Province market and comes with a land-cost advantage. If the exclusion is about management bandwidth or counterparty concentration, that is defensible; if it is by default, revisit it.

**Benchmarks that would change the recommendation:**
- If achievable bed rates fall below ~SAR 400 (Riyadh) or occupancy underwriting falls below ~80%, the ownership net yield compresses toward residential with far more risk — switch to residential.
- If the Riyadh rent freeze or a licensing reclassification is extended in a way that caps worker-housing rents, re-underwrite immediately.
- If a creditworthy multi-year anchor master lease is available at a rent-to-revenue ratio below ~60%, the lease-operate model becomes materially more attractive than ownership.

## Caveats
- **Bed rates and yields for this asset class are thinly published.** The strongest hard numbers are: the SARCC/Mawref and SARCC/Tamimi Riyadh capex disclosures (Feb 2026), the ~7.83% "Labor Housing" REIT yield (year-end 2024, an Estater proptech-blog dataset — not Tier-1 consultancy — and its paired 33.64% "cap rate" is anomalous and disregarded here), Mnzil's published bed-rate and FM-fee ranges (2026), GASTAT/Mahad labour data, and SEC/ECRA electricity tariffs. Everything labelled "derived" or "estimate" is my calculation and should be validated against a live deal.
- **No Tier-1 consultancy** (JLL, Knight Frank, CBRE, Colliers, Savills) publishes an asset-level cap rate or capex/bed for Saudi worker accommodation; JLL's only dedicated content is regulatory. Any single blended "yield" figure for this sector would be misleading — ownership cap rates and lease-operate cash-on-cash returns are not comparable and are kept separate above.
- **The SARCC projects are non-binding Heads of Terms with first phases targeted around Q1 2029** — they establish capex benchmarks, not yet operating performance.
- **No Dammam/Khobar-specific bed-rate, occupancy, capex or yield series was found**; Eastern Province figures here are inferred from portal evidence and Riyadh benchmarks and should be confirmed locally.
- Worker accommodation is an **operating business**. Its headline yield includes a large service-revenue and service-cost component; comparing it to residential rent without stripping opex will overstate its attractiveness.

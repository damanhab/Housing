# Startup Summary & Opportunity

**Last updated:** 6 October 2026
**Revision log:** 2026-10-06: added the comparison with Mnzil; stopped treating the background research as fixed; added the housing-allowance rule and both enforcement layers (Qiwa services and CR).

## The startup in bullets (draft v2)

- **What:** A Saudi **workforce-living** brand: modern, community-style neighbourhoods for blue-collar crews that companies rent per worker. **Front-stage promise:** a better-rested, happier, more stable crew, and one less headache for the employer. **Back-stage:** every bed is licensed and matched to Qiwa automatically. Compliance is built in and mentioned on the website, but it is not the headline (founder decision, 6 Oct 2026).
- **Customer:** Establishments with 20+ workers. This now includes companies that only pay a housing allowance, because **an allowance does not exempt them from the licence** (Al-Watan, Dec 2025). The buyer is the HR or government-relations lead.
- **Why now:**
  - Non-compliance **freezes Qiwa services** (no new visas, no worker transfers). That hurts immediately, before any CR suspension.
  - MOMAH says the **CR is suspended** where no valid licence exists.
  - Enforcement is moving from large to medium to small establishments. The September 2026 update gives existing housing one year to correct, which sets a deadline around Q3 2027.
- **Revenue:** bed spread (lease at ~SAR 200–400, sell at ~SAR 400–450), service fees, landlord management fees, and possibly compliance fees.
- **Model:** asset-light (lease and operate) first.

## How different are we from Mnzil? Honest answer: not different enough yet

| Our draft v1 element | Does Mnzil already do it? |
|---|---|
| Compliant beds on demand | **Yes.** It is their core product: 35k workers in 32 cities. |
| Serving SMEs | **Yes, at least in marketing.** They claim up to 60% savings for SMEs and even rent single beds to individuals. |
| Taking over landlords' buildings | **Yes.** "Operate My Unit" (تشغيل العقارات) is exactly this. |
| Transport and catering | **Yes.** |
| Compliance dashboard (licensed capacity vs. Qiwa headcount, renewals, proof certificates) | **Not visible.** Their site says only "licensed and compliant". This is the one real gap we found. |
| Asset-light model | They started there and are now adding a SAR 1bn development fund. |

**Conclusion:** draft v1 was about 80% the same as Mnzil. Mnzil is 2+ years ahead, has Founders Fund money and blue-chip clients, so a smaller copy loses. We need to differ on one sharp axis, not do the same things slightly better.

## Where we could actually be different (options)

**A. Compliance-first ("we sell the licence, not the bed")**
- Product: a compliance service and platform that manages licences, proof certificates, capacity vs. Qiwa matching, Civil Defense renewals and inspection readiness for **any** company. That includes companies whose beds sit with Mnzil, with a landlord, or in their own building.
- Why it's different: the buyer is the compliance or government-relations lead, and the fear is the Qiwa freeze or CR suspension. Mnzil sells convenience and cost.
- Risk: willingness to pay for "just compliance" is unproven, and the government could simplify Balady until the service isn't needed.

**B. Supply-side converter ("we make existing buildings licensable")**
- Product: specialise in converted buildings (عمائر سكن عمال). We run the capacity calculation, retrofit (sanitary ratios, isolation room, fire systems), handle Civil Defense approval, get the licence, then fill and operate the building.
- Why it's different: Mnzil's capital and attention are moving to new-build compounds (first deliveries ~2027–28), while most existing stock still has to be licensed before ~Q3 2027.
- Risk: operationally heavy, and it overlaps with "Operate My Unit".

**C. Geographic or vertical focus**
- Option 1: the Eastern Province industrial belt (Jubail, Ras Al Khair, SPARK). Mnzil's fund doesn't list Jubail, and there are 90k+ industrial workers there with RCJY tenders on authority-allocated land.
- Option 2: one vertical, such as contracting, cleaning/FM or food service, with housing built around that vertical's shift patterns.
- Risk: smaller total market and concentration on a few counterparties.

**Current direction (updated 6 Oct 2026):** the founder judged compliance-led positioning too weak for customers and investors. Compliance stays an **operational strength in the background** (option A capabilities built into the product), while the **brand leads with workforce living and community**: crew wellbeing, retention and productivity for employers, and a new "workforce living" category for investors. Option B (converting existing buildings) remains the supply strategy. The differentiation question against Mnzil is **open again**; we still need a sharp front-stage difference beyond brand.

## Key facts we rely on (current)

- Enforcement: Qiwa services freeze plus CR suspension; fines of only SAR 600–5,000. See `background/regulation-block-a.md` §2.
- Licensed capacity is **calculated** (4 m² per person, max 10 per room, 1 sanitary set per 8, 1-year licence), so many converted buildings will lose beds when licensed.
- Market price for a serviced bed ≈ SAR 400–450 per month (Mnzil ~$100–120).
- Scale: ~1m licensed beneficiaries so far, against ~8.8m non-Saudi workers and a ~3.4m construction workforce.

## Next validation steps

- Interview 15–20 SMEs (20–150 workers): has Qiwa been frozen or flagged, what do they pay today, and **would they pay for compliance alone?** (This tests option A.)
- Interview 10 landlords of converted buildings: share of buildings that can be licensed, capacity loss, appetite for a management deal. (This tests option B.)
- Get a Mnzil quote as a prospective customer, and ask how they handle the proof certificate and capacity matching.
- Read the gazetted MOMAH conditions end to end.
